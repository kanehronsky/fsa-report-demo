        Suggested

        How the numbers are worked out. A student is “working on” an item if it was one of their teaching targets at their first assessment of the year: a gap below their level, or one of the three items where the assessment stopped. The item counts as reached if they scored 100% on it at their last assessment that year.

        Items that are hard everywhere

        How often students across the whole program reached 100% on each item they were working on. An item that is low in most schools may point to the item, its resources or the training, rather than to any one school. Only items with at least 40 students working on them are shown.

        Reach rateThe 4 lowest

          Who can see this. Only program administrators. School staff can see their own school, year groups, classes and students, but nothing from other schools, including these comparisons.

          An item is only flagged when a school is behind by more than chance would explain, given how many students were working on it. For example, 1 of 4 students against a program average of 50% isn't flagged, but 3 of 12 is.

          A flag or a “Check in” doesn't say why a school is behind. It's a prompt to look closer and offer training or support where it would help.

          At a glance

          Quick numbers and things worth a closer look.

          Suggested

            Item history

            Every item reached, at every assessment. Darker means a higher score. An orange outline marks where that assessment stopped.

            Suggested

            How the numbers are worked out. Total % is the sum of all 36 item scores divided by 3,600. Items not reached count as 0, so the total rises as the student gets further through the assessment.

            Current level is the last item scored at 100% before the three items in a row that stopped the assessment.

            There are two versions of the assessment, Standard and AAC. A student starts on one and stays on it. The label at the top says which.
